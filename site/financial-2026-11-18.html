Cardiovascular Reactivity to Peer Rejection Moderates the Impact of Early Life Stress on Body Mass Index in Adolescent Girls: A Cluster Analysis Approach.

Biological psychology · 2026-11-17

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

Early life stress is linked to poor cardiometabolic outcomes including obesity. Blunted physiological stress reactivity, which may be adaptive in adverse contexts, has also been associated with obesity. The present study examines whether cardiovascular reactivity (CVR) serves as a biological susceptibility factor that moderates the longitudinal association between early life stress and body mass index (BMI) in female adolescents. Participants were 80 female adolescents (M age = 13.3 years). Participants completed the Psychosocial Schedule to assess cumulative early life stress. The Yale Interpersonal Stressor-Child paradigm was executed to assess systolic blood pressure (SBP), diastolic blood pressure (DBP), and heart rate (HR) during acute stress exposure. K-means clustering identified CVR patterns based on reactivity from baseline to the stressor. Height and weight were measured at baseline and twelve months later to derive BMI-for-age. Two CVR clusters were identified: high CVR (n=31; M &#x394;SBP =15.0mmHg, M &#x394;DBP =8.21mmHg, M &#x394;HR =7.05 bpm) and low CVR (n= 49; M &#x394;SBP =4.70mmHg, M &#x394;DBP =3.58mmHg, M &#x394;HR =0.94 bpm). CVR moderated the association between early life stress and BMI-for-age at 12-month follow-up, controlling for pubertal status, socioeconomic status, depression, and race/ethnicity (B(SE) =-4.61 (1.71), p =.009). Higher early life stress predicted higher BMI-for-age among adolescents with low CVR (B(SE)=3.53 (1.39), p=.01) but not among those with high CVR. Early life stress was associated with higher BMI-for-age only among adolescents with blunted CVR, suggesting reduced stress reactivity as a potential physiological risk pathway for obesity among adolescent females.

Cognitive capacity in subjective cognitive decline: Insights from electrophysiological markers and dual-task performance.

Biological psychology · 2026-11-17

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

Subjective cognitive decline (SCD) refers to self-reported cognitive deficits in the absence of objective cognitive impairment. Evidence suggests that neural changes may precede performance declines, highlighting the potential of electroencephalography (EEG) to detect early cognitive declines. Electrophysiological markers and changes in cognitive and motor performance were examined during dual-task performance to better understand the neural mechanisms underlying SCD. Older adults with (n=20) and without (n=20) SCD performed a working memory and finger tapping task simultaneously (i.e., dual task) and alone (i.e., single cognitive or motor). Linear mixed models were used to assess task and group effects for behavioural performance and EEG measures, including mean amplitude and power spectral density, in frontal, central, and parietal regions of interest (ROI). P2 and P3b mean amplitude were smaller during the dual task compared to single cognitive task. Additionally, the SCD group exhibited higher theta power overall and higher alpha power in each task and ROI compared to the non-SCD group. Higher power was observed during the dual task compared to single tasks except in the alpha band where the opposite was observed. Cognitive and motor performance decreased between single and dual tasks, and slower finger tapping speed was observed in the SCD compared to the non-SCD group. Neural and performance findings support the capacity sharing model of attention and may reflect the use of compensatory mechanisms in the SCD group. Electrophysiological changes may precede objective cognitive deficits and have the potential to complement existing approaches to characterizing SCD but must be examined longitudinally.
