Spontaneously emerging patterns show category-biased similarity to task-evoked visual patterns beyond human visual cortex.

NeuroImage · 2026-11-19

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

In the absence of any task, spontaneous brain activity exhibits patterns that resemble those evoked by natural stimuli and behaviors. This raises the possibility that resting-state activity patterns may relate to maintenance and consolidation of behaviorally relevant information states. Using functional MRI (fMRI) data from 100 healthy adults in the Human Connectome Project, we investigated whether neural signals evoked by specific visual stimuli (body parts, faces, places, tools) show similarity to resting-state activity across non-canonical visual areas, i.e., motor and association cortices. Participants underwent a working memory task and resting-state scanning. We analyzed multivoxel pattern similarity between task-evoked activations and spontaneous activity in bilateral hand motor regions and attention-related networks. Spatial correlations between task-evoked multivoxel patterns for each visual category and frame-by-frame resting-state signals were computed, with the upper 90% distribution percentile (U90) as a similarity metric. We found that resting-state patterns in the left hemisphere hand motor region showed significantly higher similarity to tool-evoked patterns than place-evoked patterns, consistent with the interpretation that tools recruit motor affordances and hand&#x2011;object associations in motor cortex. In the bilateral dorsal attention network, resting-task similarity for non-face evoked patterns significantly exceeded that for faces. Moreover, effects localized to posterior dorsal attention network, aligning with the dorsal visual pathway's role in spatial-action coding of behaviorally relevant objects. These results suggest that resting-state activity in non-visual cortices shows category-biased similarity to task-evoked visual patterns, consistent with the view that spontaneous neural dynamics preferentially reactivate ecologically salient stimuli.

Short-video experience and brain-state dynamics during the processing of rapidly changing video information in youth.

NeuroImage · 2026-11-19

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

Short-video platforms frequently expose youth to rapidly changing video information. Processing such inputs requires large-scale brain networks to reorganize with the ongoing context. However, little is known about these dynamics or their association with prior short-video viewing experience. A total of 108 youth aged 8-18 years completed resting-state and naturalistic movie-viewing fMRI scans. The movie comprised rapidly changing and relatively continuous segments. Hidden Markov models were applied to 17 cortical networks to identify recurrent brain states, and a joint rest-task analysis examined the context specificity of the main finding. Eight task-related brain states were identified. Five differed in fractional occupancy (FO) between conditions, and global switching was slightly higher during rapidly changing segments. State 7 was the only state showing an interaction with short-video viewing experience. Its FO was higher during relatively continuous than rapidly changing segments among participants reporting no prior Douyin use, whereas the condition difference was not significant among those reporting prior Douyin use. Visit frequency analyses showed a similar pattern. The interaction remained robust after covariate adjustment and was also observed in the joint rest-task analysis, with no significant group difference at rest. Rapidly changing video information was associated with brain-state reorganization in youth. Douyin use was associated with lower State 7 occupancy during relatively continuous narrative segments; no significant group difference in the matched state's occupancy was detected at rest. These findings provide new evidence linking short-video viewing experience to context-dependent brain dynamics in youth.

Brain-heart decoupling in somatic depression: Desynchronization between salience network neuropsychological connectivity and heart rate variability.

NeuroImage · 2026-11-19

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

Somatic depression is a distinct subtype of major depressive disorder, with a poorer response to treatment, an elevated risk of suicidal behaviour, and a worse prognosis. Interoception, the sense of internal bodily signals, is related to somatic symptoms. However, the underlying pathophysiological mechanisms of body brain communication remain unclear. 293 depressed patients (145 with somatic symptoms and 148 without), and 160 healthy controls underwent magnetoencephalography during a resting state. Both the intra- and inter-network neural oscillation patterns of the salience network, were computed, alongside and heart rate variability (HRV) as a measure of autonomic function. Individuals with somatic depression exhibited lower HRV, accompanied by theta hyper-connectivity within the salience network. Although higher theta connectivity was linked to increased HRV in controls, this correlation was attenuated or even reversed in patients with somatic depression. Our findings identify a specific pattern of brain-heart decoupling in somatic depression, characterized by disrupted communication between salience network theta oscillations and autonomic cardiac regulation. This provides novel neuroimaging evidence for body-brain communication deficits in a clinically important depression subtype.

Training interbrain synchrony through dyadic neurofeedback modulates empathy-for-pain.

NeuroImage · 2026-11-19

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

Dyadic neurofeedback offers a socially grounded approach for modulating neural processes that unfold between interacting individuals. While recent work demonstrates that dyadic neurofeedback can enhance inter-brain synchrony during interaction, whether its effects extend beyond the trained dyad remains unclear. Here, we examined whether dyadic neurofeedback targeting inter-brain synchrony in the inferior frontal gyrus (IFG) is associated with changes in empathy-for-pain toward unfamiliar social targets. One hundred and eighty-six participants, randomly assigned to dyads, completed three neurofeedback training sessions within one week. Feedback was contingent on real-time IFG inter-brain synchrony in the neurofeedback group or on prerecorded signals in the sham-feedback control group. Empathy-for-pain was assessed before and after training. IFG inter-brain synchrony increased significantly across sessions in the neurofeedback group but not in the control group. Empathy-for-pain ratings showed different trajectories across conditions in the primary raw-data analysis, decreasing significantly in the sham-feedback group while remaining relatively stable in the neurofeedback group. A secondary analysis adjusting for a previously observed task-specific test-retest decline showed a significant increase in the neurofeedback group. Individuals with lower baseline trait empathy showed larger training-related increases in IFG inter-brain synchrony. Network analyses suggested broader changes involving IFG-IPL connectivity. Structural equation modeling further identified significant associations among training-related intra-brain synchrony, inter-brain synchrony, and empathy-for-pain change, consistent with the proposed relationship among these processes. Together, the findings indicate that dyadic neurofeedback can modulate IFG inter-brain synchrony across repeated training and may influence the trajectory of empathy-for-pain responding beyond the immediate training partner.

Desync and connect: Noninvasive MEG evidence of long-range coupling between locally desynchronized regions.

NeuroImage · 2026-11-19

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

A prevailing assumption in systems neuroscience is that long-range phase coupling between cortical regions requires locally synchronized oscillatory activity within each interacting area. Here we ask whether distal phase coupling can be detected from MEG data when both interacting regions undergo concurrent event-related desynchronization (ERD). Using controlled simulations with progressively disrupted local phase coherence, we first establish that the Context-Dependent PSIICOS (CD-PSIICOS) framework - a projection-based connectivity estimator that separates power-dominated spatial leakage from interaction-specific cross-spectral structure - can recover distal coupling under increasing local phase dispersion, whereas estimators based on the imaginary part of the cross-spectrum fail at near-zero lags. We then analysed recordings of a center-out reaching dataset from nine participants. A group decomposition of the coupling time courses identifies four long-range networks in alpha (8-13 Hz) and two in beta (15-25 Hz), each with its own time window. For each network we follow the local connections between its own sources as the power-dominated subspace is projected out, separating leakage-driven from leakage-resistant local coupling. In the earliest alpha network the leakage-resistant pairs stay at or below their pre-stimulus level at every rank while the power of the same sources falls, so this interhemispheric interaction may have no stable local counterpart. In the beta networks such a counterpart does survive, but the coupling it carries is an order of magnitude weaker than the long-range coupling of the same sources. Only in the latest alpha window, by which time power has returned to baseline, do local and long-range coupling rise together. Factorizing the coupling into the amplitude of the single-trial cross-terms and the consistency of their phases separates the same cases: in the networks without a local counterpart, phase consistency increases inside the window while the amplitude of the cross-terms declines, whereas in the network whose local coupling rises with it, the increase reflects a growth of the cross-terms themselves rather than any improvement in their phase agreement. Long-range phase coupling can therefore persist, and in places strengthen, between regions undergoing macroscopic ERD, and noninvasive MEG can resolve this dissociation when leakage-aware estimation is employed.
