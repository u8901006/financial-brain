Decolonizing mental health: the Nova Scotia transcultural mental health program as a model of care.

BMC health services research · 2026-11-11

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

African Nova Scotians, Indigenous peoples, and refugees experience significant disparities in access to culturally appropriate mental health care due to systemic inequities, intergenerational trauma, stigma, and limited availability of culturally competent services. The Nova Scotia Transcultural Mental Health Program (NSTMHP) was established to close gaps in serving these equity-denied groups using a culturally responsive and collaborative model of care in primary healthcare settings. This paper describes the development, implementation, governance, and model of care of the NSTMHP as an approach to advancing equitable, decolonized mental health services. This descriptive program-development paper outlines the collaborative processes used to design and implement the NSTMHP. The program was developed through partnerships involving the Dalhousie University Department of Psychiatry, Nova Scotia Health Mental Health and Addictions Program, culturally specific community organizations, patient advocates, and primary healthcare clinics. A shared-care model was implemented in which culturally competent psychiatrists provided direct and indirect psychiatric consultations within primary care settings. Governance structures, service pathways, implementation processes, educational initiatives, and research activities were reviewed descriptively. No statistical analyses were conducted, as this paper focuses on program design, implementation, and model development. The NSTMHP established a structured governance framework consisting of an advisory council and management team representing healthcare, academic, and community partners. The program developed culturally responsive pathways for psychiatric consultation services for African Nova Scotians, Indigenous peoples, and refugees within community-based primary care clinics. Key outcomes included improved collaboration between psychiatry and primary care, enhanced opportunities for culturally informed education and research, increased integration of mental health services within equity-denied communities and strengthened community partnerships. Important implementation challenges included sustaining funding, recruiting and retaining culturally competent clinicians, addressing social and structural determinants of health, and adapting assessment and treatment approaches to align with diverse cultural perspectives and decolonizing principles. The NSTMHP demonstrates the feasibility and potential value of a collaborative, culturally responsive, and community-informed model for improving access to mental health services among equity-denied populations. The program provides a promising framework for integrating transcultural and decolonizing approaches into mental health systems and may inform the development of similar equity-oriented initiatives in other jurisdictions.

Cost-effectiveness of digital health in primary care: a systematic review.

BMC health services research · 2026-11-11

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

Digital health has rapidly expanded, accelerated by the COVID-19 pandemic, and is widely used for consultations, disease management, and efficiency improvements in primary care. While it is considered clinically effective and potentially cost-effective, evidence on economic impact remains limited. Previous reviews have been condition-specific, highlighting the need for a systematic review focused on cost-effectiveness of digital health within primary care. The aim of this systematic review was to summarize and investigate the cost-effectiveness of digital health among adult primary care patients. A systematic literature search was conducted using the PICO framework. Studies were included if they involved adult primary care patients (&#x2265;18&#x2009;years), evaluated digital health interventions versus usual care, and reported cost&#x2011;effectiveness based on economic evaluations. PubMed, Scopus, and Health Business Elite were searched in January 2025. An updated search was conducted in September 2026. Results were reported in a Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) flow diagram. Methodological quality was assessed using the Joanna Briggs Institute Checklist for Economic Evaluations. A narrative synthesis was performed. The initial search identified 4,091 records and resulted in 47 included studies. The updated search in September 2026 identified an additional 658 records, of which seven additional studies met the eligibility criteria, resulting in a total of 54 included studies. Most were trial&#x2011;based full economic evaluations focusing on digital interventions for depression and anxiety, adopting a healthcare perspective. Overall, digital health interventions were frequently associated with favourable economic outcomes, although results varied by intervention, population, economic evaluation method, and time horizon. This review is innovative in its focus on the cost&#x2011;effectiveness of digital health interventions in primary care. Unlike previous reviews, it systematically synthesises trial&#x2011; and model&#x2011;based economic evaluations. The review advances the field by clarifying the economic evidence base and has practical implications for decision&#x2011;makers considering the implementation of digital health in primary care. The review is limited by rapidly evolving digital health terminology, the exclusion of grey literature, and substantial methodological heterogeneity between trial&#x2011;based and model&#x2011;based economic evaluations. Potential selection bias related to digital literacy may limit generalisability. Digital health in primary care demonstrated favourable economic outcomes across a broad range of clinical areas, with the majority of studies reporting cost-effective results. However, despite this positive pattern, methodological heterogeneity limits comparability between studies. Greater standardization of economic evaluation methods and reporting is therefore needed to strengthen the evidence base and support implementation decisions. PROSPERO registration no. CRD42025644904; [https://www.crd.york.ac.uk/PROSPERO/view/CRD42025644904].

The temporal representation of computational features from body expressions.

Social cognitive and affective neuroscience · 2026-11-11

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

Body expression is an important channel for human communication. To understand the underlying perceptual mechanisms, the present study used electroencephalography and time-resolved multivariate analyses to track the temporal emergence of computationally defined body features and their contribution to emotion representations. Six body postural features (i.e., limb angles, symmetry, shoulder ratio, surface, potential energy, and limb contraction) were computed based on fourteen body joints. Representational similarity analysis revealed that the body surface and potential energy were significantly encoded at an early stage, persisting for an extended duration over parietal- posterior electrodes. In contrast, limb contraction was encoded at a later stage and exhibited a shorter duration. Furthermore, commonality analysis demonstrated that the postural features of surface and potential energy explained the neural variance of emotion categories, with body surface accounting for the largest proportion of the shared neural variance. These findings provide empirical evidence for the temporal dynamics of computationally defined body-posture representations during emotional body perception and may inform future computational models of body expression perception.

Feedback Processing in Individual and Dyadic Group Risk Decisions Under Gain-Loss Framing: Evidence from FRN and P3b.

Biological psychology · 2026-11-11

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

Decision-making in social contexts is influenced by gain-loss framing and interpersonal responsibility, yet how these factors jointly shape the neural dynamics of outcome evaluation remains unclear. Using event-related potentials (ERPs) and a dyadic turn-taking Balloon Analogue Risk Task (BART), this study examined how decision context (individual vs. dyadic group), gain-loss framing, and feedback valence influence risky choice and feedback processing. Participants completed the task in both individual and dyadic contexts while EEG was recorded. Behaviorally, participants exhibited greater risk-taking in the dyadic group than in the individual context and showed reduced risk-taking following negative relative to positive feedback. ERP analyses revealed significant interactions between decision context and feedback valence for both feedback-related negativity (FRN) and P3b, suggesting that decision context modulated neural responses associated with both early and later stages of outcome evaluation. Specifically, FRN responses to negative feedback were enhanced in the dyadic context, whereas P3b responses were larger in the individual context. The dyadic context also enhanced neural differentiation between positive and negative feedback. In addition, P3b showed a descriptive pattern consistent with a possible interaction between decision context and gain-loss framing, although this interaction did not reach statistical significance after correction for multiple comparisons. These findings demonstrate that social decision context shapes both behavioral risk-taking and the neural dynamics of feedback processing during interactive sequential decision-making. The study extends previous research by providing neurophysiological evidence from a real dyadic interactive paradigm, highlighting how social context influences outcome evaluation under risk.
