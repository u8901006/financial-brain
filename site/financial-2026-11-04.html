Training Interbrain Synchrony through Dyadic Neurofeedback Modulates Empathy-for-Pain.

NeuroImage · 2026-11-03

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

Dyadic neurofeedback offers a socially grounded approach for modulating neural processes that unfold between interacting individuals. While recent work demonstrates that dyadic neurofeedback can enhance inter-brain synchrony during interaction, whether its effects extend beyond the trained dyad remains unclear. Here, we examined whether dyadic neurofeedback targeting inter-brain synchrony in the inferior frontal gyrus (IFG) is associated with changes in empathy-for-pain toward unfamiliar social targets. One hundred and eighty-six participants, randomly assigned to dyads, completed three neurofeedback training sessions within one week. Feedback was contingent on real-time IFG inter-brain synchrony in the neurofeedback group or on prerecorded signals in the sham-feedback control group. Empathy-for-pain was assessed before and after training. IFG inter-brain synchrony increased significantly across sessions in the neurofeedback group but not in the control group. Empathy-for-pain ratings showed different trajectories across conditions in the primary raw-data analysis, decreasing significantly in the sham-feedback group while remaining relatively stable in the neurofeedback group. A secondary analysis adjusting for a previously observed task-specific test-retest decline showed a significant increase in the neurofeedback group. Individuals with lower baseline trait empathy showed larger training-related increases in IFG inter-brain synchrony. Network analyses suggested broader changes involving IFG-IPL connectivity. Structural equation modeling further identified significant associations among training-related intra-brain synchrony, inter-brain synchrony, and empathy-for-pain change, consistent with the proposed relationship among these processes. Together, the findings indicate that dyadic neurofeedback can modulate IFG inter-brain synchrony across repeated training and may influence the trajectory of empathy-for-pain responding beyond the immediate training partner.

Desync and connect: Noninvasive MEG evidence of long-range coupling between locally desynchronized regions.

NeuroImage · 2026-11-03

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

A prevailing assumption in systems neuroscience is that long-range phase coupling between cortical regions requires locally synchronized oscillatory activity within each interacting area. Here we ask whether distal phase coupling can be detected from MEG data when both interacting regions undergo concurrent event-related desynchronization (ERD). Using controlled simulations with progressively disrupted local phase coherence, we first establish that the Context-Dependent PSIICOS (CD-PSIICOS) framework - a projection-based connectivity estimator that separates power-dominated spatial leakage from interaction-specific cross-spectral structure - can recover distal coupling under increasing local phase dispersion, whereas estimators based on the imaginary part of the cross-spectrum fail at near-zero lags. We then analyzed recordings of a center-out reaching dataset from nine participants. A group decomposition of the coupling time courses identifies four long-range networks in alpha (8-13 Hz) and two in beta (15-25 Hz), each with its own time window. For each network we follow the local connections between its own sources as the power-dominated subspace is projected out, separating leakage-driven from leakage-resistant local coupling. In the earliest alpha network the leakage-resistant pairs stay at or below their pre-stimulus level at every rank while the power of the same sources falls, so this interhemispheric interaction may have no stable local counterpart. In the beta networks such a counterpart does survive, but the coupling it carries is an order of magnitude weaker than the long-range coupling of the same sources. Only in the latest alpha window, by which time power has returned to baseline, do local and long-range coupling rise together. Factorising the coupling into the amplitude of the single-trial cross-terms and the consistency of their phases separates the same cases: in the networks without a local counterpart, phase consistency increases inside the window while the amplitude of the cross-terms declines, whereas in the network whose local coupling rises with it, the increase reflects a growth of the cross-terms themselves rather than any improvement in their phase agreement. Long-range phase coupling can therefore persist, and in places strengthen, between regions undergoing macroscopic ERD, and noninvasive MEG can resolve this dissociation when leakage-aware estimation is employed.
