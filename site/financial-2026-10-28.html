The Effects of Health Shocks on Time Spent in Home Production.

Health economics · 2026-10-26

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

This paper examines the causal impact of health shocks on time spent in home production among retired individuals. Two channels with opposite predictions motivate the analysis: health shocks may tighten household budgets, inducing substitution toward home-produced goods (income effect), or they may reduce the physical and functional capacity to perform domestic tasks (impairing effect). Using longitudinal data from the Health and Retirement Study, I estimate condition-specific causal effects of ten health conditions on home production in a staggered difference-in-differences design. Conditions that impair functional capacity, such as psychiatric conditions, depression on the Center for Epidemiological Studies Depression (CES-D) scale, and adverse self-reported health, reduce home production substantially. Psychiatric conditions reduce weekly home production by 3.2&#xa0;hours, a 16% decline; CES-D depression and self-reported health shocks each reduce it by approximately 1&#xa0;hour per week. Conditions that raise out-of-pocket medical costs but impose modest functional limitations produce no increase. The evidence therefore supports the impairing effect and gives no support to the income effect. The decline is not fully offset by increased help utilization or by spending on market substitutes such as housekeeping services, prepared meals, and yard work. Health shocks thus impose welfare costs beyond what monetary measures alone capture, with direct implications for the design of home- and community-based support services for retirees.

Factors associated with communicative and financial barriers to healthcare utilization among people with selected citizenships in Germany: results from a nationwide cross-sectional study.

BMC health services research · 2026-10-27

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

Research has shown that migrants in Germany face various barriers to healthcare access, including communicative and financial barriers. This study examined which sociodemographic, migration-related, and psychosocial factors are associated with these barriers in order to better understand lower healthcare utilization among migrants. We used data from the nationwide cross-sectional health interview survey GEDA Fokus, which included people with Croatian, Italian, Polish, Syrian, or Turkish citizenship living in Germany. Descriptive, univariable, and multivariable analyses were conducted separately for communicative and financial barriers while accounting for relevant social factors. Of the 6,038 participants, 16.2% reported communicative barriers and 9.0% reported financial barriers to healthcare utilization. According to the regression analyses, gender, age, German language proficiency, and self-reported experiences of racist discrimination were associated with communicative barriers. For financial barriers, associations with gender, educational level, and experiences of discrimination due to the socioeconomic situation were found. The results indicate that demographic, socioeconomic, migration-related, and psychosocial factors are associated with communicative and financial barriers to healthcare utilization. Gender and experiences of discrimination were significantly associated with both communicative and financial barriers. In practice, the provision of qualified translation and interpretation services, anti-discrimination and awareness training for healthcare staff, and the expansion of low-threshold, financially accessible services could help promote more equitable access to healthcare among migrants.

Within-family heritability estimates for behavioural and disease phenotypes from 500,000 sibling pairs of diverse ancestries.

Nature human behaviour · 2026-10-27

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

Quantification of the direct effect of genetic variation on human behavioural traits is important for understanding between-individual variation in socioeconomic and health outcomes but estimates of their heritability can be biased by between-family indirect genetic effects. By contrast, using within-family variation in DNA sharing is robust to most confounding factors including shared environmental effects and population stratification. Yet, accurate estimates for most traits are not available using this design, and none for non-European ancestry populations. Here we analyse approximately 500,000 sibling pairs with diverse genetic ancestries and obtain within-family heritability estimates for 14 phenotypes, including two well-studied model traits: height (0.70&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;0.03) and BMI (0.49&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;0.03). For behaviour, we find significant within-family heritability for smoking initiation (0.34&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;0.05), alcohol consumption (0.18&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;0.04), number of children (0.27&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;0.11) and personality ('talk versus listen', 0.48&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;0.13). In addition, we estimated large heritability for type 2 diabetes (0.43&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;0.06) and asthma (0.34&#x2009;&#xb1;&#x2009;0.06), whose risk factors include behavioural traits. Overall, we show concordant estimates across ancestry groups and highlight a significant contribution of shared environmental effects for behaviour and type 2 diabetes risk, which may have inflated between-family estimates. Altogether, our results provide within-family evidence that genetic variation is associated with variation in complex traits, common disease and exposures. Our heritability estimates are concordant across genetic ancestries, lower than those from twin-based studies, yet larger than has been accounted for by genome-wide association studies so far.
