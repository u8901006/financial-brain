Why are voluntary health insurance holders willing to pay tax towards public healthcare? A qualitative study from Sweden.

BMC health services research · 2026-09-30

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

Voluntary health insurance (VHI) is increasing in many European healthcare systems, including the tax-funded public healthcare systems found in Northern Europe. As private alternatives to publicly financed health services expand, there is a risk that the willingness to pay tax towards redistributive public healthcare systems will diminish. This study investigated how employer-funded VHI holders accounted for their continued willingness to pay income tax to suppor the public healthcare system and the motivation underlying their tax compliance. A qualitative approach based on 19 semi-structured interviews with individuals holding an employer-funded VHI in Sweden was used. The interviews were analysed using thematic analysis. The findings suggest a continued willingness to pay tax towards the public healthcare system among Swedes with an employer-funded VHI. The support was motivated by a moral logic and belief in the principle of equal access to health for all citizens. Respondents also expressed ambivalence about gaining faster access to care than others. These accounts suggest that the growth of employer-funded VHI does not by itself erode the willingness to fund public healthcare, and that the egalitarian principles underpinning a universal system can sustain a sense of obligation to contribute even among those who use private healthcare. The ambivalence respondents expressed indicates that contributing on egalitarian grounds and benefiting from privileged access are not easily reconciled.

Between support and uncertainty: employers' perspectives on workplace inclusion of people with mental health problems.

BMC health services research · 2026-09-30

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

Despite increasing awareness of mental health in the workplace, people with mental health problems continue to face substantial barriers to labour market participation. While stigma-related explanations have been widely addressed, less is known about how employers respond to mental health problems within the constraints of everyday organisational practice. This study explores employers' perspectives on workplace inclusion, with a particular focus on ambivalences, facilitating orientations, and expectations towards employees with mental health problems. This study reports findings from the first, qualitative phase of a participatory mixed-methods research project conducted in Southern Germany. Between November 2022 and August 2023, we conducted 29 semi-structured interviews with employers from companies across different sectors. Data were analysed using structured qualitative content analysis. Employers described ambivalent orientations towards the inclusion of people with mental health problems. While mental health issues were seen as increasingly normalised and framed within broader societal developments, employers reported uncertainties regarding work organisation, team functioning, and predictability. Facilitating orientations included social responsibility, recognition of the health-promoting role of employment, pragmatic responses to labour shortages, and positive prior experiences. At the same time, workplace participation was often described as depending on employees' transparency regarding work-related limitations, individual responsibility for managing mental health, and disclosure as a prerequisite for employers' support and mutual trust. Workplace inclusion of people with mental health problems appears to depend not only on employers' attitudes but also on practical considerations related to work organization and team functioning. Employers' perspectives reflected ambivalence, balancing commitments to participation with concerns about predictability, fairness, and collective functioning. Expectations regarding disclosure, responsibility, and transparency were central to perceived feasibility of support. Addressing these organisational dynamics may help inform more context-sensitive strategies to promote sustainable labour market participation.

Interoceptive Awareness is Associated with Enhanced Neural Response to Unpleasant Images.

Social cognitive and affective neuroscience · 2026-09-29

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

Interoception, or perception of internal bodily signals, is thought to be an important contributor to emotional experience and cognition, and individual differences in interoceptive beliefs may affect cognitive processing of emotional information. Addressing this issue, the present study examined the correlation between self-reported interoceptive awareness and EEG markers of motivated attention to emotional versus neutral images. A sample of undergraduates (n = 115) viewed images of neutral, pleasant, and unpleasant scenes while EEG was recorded, and the late positive potential (LPP) was extracted for each image type. Group results replicate prior findings of enhanced LPP signals for pleasant and unpleasant compared to neutral images. Moreover, a greater degree of enhanced LPP for unpleasant images (versus pleasant or neutral images) was positively correlated with self-reported interoceptive awareness. Results support the role of interoceptive beliefs in cortical processing of emotional information.
