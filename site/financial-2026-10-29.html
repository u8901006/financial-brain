Exploring healthcare professionals' views and experiences of having access to a rehabilitation coordinator in their work in psychiatric care: a qualitative descriptive study.

BMC health services research · 2026-10-28

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

In Sweden, rehabilitation coordinators (RCs) have been introduced into healthcare service to offer coordination and support to patients, with the aim of streamlining sick leave and rehabilitation processes and promoting patients' return-to-work (RTW)/entry into work. Before the introduction of RCs, there was no corresponding function with mainly this specific focus and responsibility. The present study explores healthcare professionals' (HCPs') views and experiences of having access to a RC within psychiatric care. This descriptive qualitative study involved individual semi-structured interviews with 12 HCPs in psychiatric care. Data were analysed using inductive thematic analysis. Two main themes were identified: "A specific function that improves the ability to focus on and support vocational rehabilitation" and "Uncertainty and confusion regarding roles and contributions within vocational rehabilitation". The introduction of a RC was viewed positively, enhancing the possibilities to focus on and offer patients coordination and vocational rehabilitation (VR) efforts. The HCPs felt they lacked both the time and knowledge to manage these tasks within their current work situation. By sharing knowledge, taking responsibility for, and coordinating the patients' VR process, the RC was seen as a valuable resource that contributed to better working situation for HCPs. However, the introduction of the RC also brought about uncertainty and confusion, particularly linked to the RC function and the role of HCPs within VR. The RC's focus and in-depth knowledge of VR addressed a gap within psychiatric healthcare units. This contributed to providing more extensive support in patients' VR processes and improved the working environment for HCPs. To minimise inequalities in patient care and support the implementation of the RC function within psychiatric care, it is essential to clarify the roles and responsibilities of both RCs and HCPs in patients' VR processes. The findings of this study may guide and support the implementation of RCs across various care units, potentially extending beyond psychiatric care. Clinicaltrials.gov Register Platform (ID NCT03729050 November 2018).

Brief education and nudging to facilitate TENS use in postoperative care: a quasi-experimental mixed methods study.

BMC health services research · 2026-10-28

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

Implementing evidence-based practices into routine clinical care remains a challenge, with traditional implementation strategies yielding modest effects on clinical behavior. In postoperative pain management, evidence-based non-pharmacological modalities such as transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation (TENS) remain underutilized. Nudging, through subtle modifications to the choice architecture, offers a cost-effective, resource-efficient approach to enhancing adoption and sustaining action at the point of care. This study evaluated whether combining a brief educational workshop with a nudging phase using visual prompts could enhance the adoption of TENS in routine postoperative care. An explanatory sequential mixed methods design was applied, combining a quasi-experimental pre-post study with three observation points and qualitative interviews. The study was conducted in a 15-bed postoperative ward at a Swedish university hospital. The intervention comprised a 45-minute hands-on educational workshop followed by a 12-week asynchronous and passive nudging phase. Quantitative data on TENS use were collected from a logbook maintained by healthcare professionals and analyzed with descriptive statistics and comparative tests. To contextualize and explain observed practice changes, semi-structured interviews with a purposive sample of healthcare professionals (n&#x2009;=&#x2009;13) were thematically analyzed. TENS administrations increased significantly after the interventions compared to baseline. Qualitative findings showed that the interventions reframed TENS from a peripheral option to an integrated component of postoperative pain care. Nudging reinforced awareness, normalized TENS administration in everyday practice, and contributed to early routinization and maintained integration into clinical reasoning. A brief educational workshop followed by a transparent asynchronous and passive nudging strategy was associated with increased and continued documented use of TENS during the study period, in a setting where no administrations were recorded at baseline despite available devices and a local protocol. This low-resource approach might complement traditional implementation strategies by increasing the visibility of evidence-based non-pharmacological options in routine postoperative care. ClinicalTrials.gov, NCT06696430. Registered 13 November 2024, https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06696430 .

When Fakes Feel Real: Behavioral and Neural Mechanisms of Deepfake Hyperrealism in Younger and Older Adults.

Social cognitive and affective neuroscience · 2026-10-28

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

Deepfakes are designed to mimic real people and constitute an emerging exploitation threat. They are potent methods of fraud as humans detect deepfake images at near or below chance levels, and deepfakes are frequently perceived as more real even than real images (deepfake hyperrealism). Anterior insula (aINS) function is implicated in processing salient, untrustworthy cues suggesting that low aINS recruitment during deepfake perception may underlie experiences of deepfake hyperrealism. Further, aging may confer increased experiences of deepfake hyperrealism, given increased susceptibility to deception and reduced aINS sensitivity to deceptive stimuli with age. Here, 156 participants (62 younger: 18-37 years, 94 older: 60-89 years) evaluated the realness of real and deepfake face images while undergoing fMRI. Behavioral results revealed deepfake hyperrealism in both age groups, with these effects more pronounced in older adults. Our imaging results indicated lower aINS activation was associated with higher realness ratings and aINS activity was also lower for deepfake than real images in both younger and older adults. These findings provide additional support for deepfake hyperrealism in perception, underscoring the threat of deepfakes as a method of exploitation across adulthood. Results also support that muted aINS response to deepfakes constitutes a neuromechanism in deception vulnerability.

mPFC-Dependent Valuation Shapes Information Sharing: Causal Evidence from tDCS and a Dyadic Social Media Paradigm.

Social cognitive and affective neuroscience · 2026-10-28

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

Information sharing drives the spread of both accurate and misleading content, yet its psychological and neural mechanisms remain unclear. We investigated whether perceived self- and social relevance influence sharing through downstream content evaluations, and whether this process depends on medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC) function. In a dyadic social media paradigm, 186 participants were assigned to anodal, cathodal, or sham tDCS over the mPFC. Senders judged 30 science news items (15 true and 15 false) on truthfulness, self-relevance, social relevance, interestingness, and importance before deciding whether to share each item with a real receiver. Structural equation modeling showed that self- and social relevance predicted sharing indirectly through evaluations of interestingness, importance, and truthfulness. mPFC stimulation did not reliably change sharing rates, but altered evaluative processing: it reduced perceived interestingness, and anodal stimulation produced a significant negative indirect pathway to sharing through reduced interestingness. Multi-group analyses indicated that these valuation pathways were invariant across true and false news. On the receiver side, social endorsement increased reading of shared items but had little effect on post-reading evaluations. Together, these findings support a causal contribution of mPFC-targeted neuromodulation to evaluative processing relevant to sharing, with limited evidence for a direct effect on sharing behavior.
