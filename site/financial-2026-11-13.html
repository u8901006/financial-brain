Socioeconomic inequalities in healthcare utilization among older adults in Indonesia: social network mediation and dynamics before and after Universal Health Coverage.

BMC health services research · 2026-11-12

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

Socioeconomic inequalities in healthcare utilization (HCU) remain widespread in many low- and middle-income countries, even following major health system reforms. Social networks may influence individuals' ability to access healthcare by providing information, resources, and support, yet their role in shaping socioeconomic inequalities in HCU remains poorly understood. This study examined whether social networks and their dynamics over time mediate the relationship between socioeconomic position (SEP) and HCU among older adults in Indonesia across periods before and during the implementation of Universal Health Coverage. Data were drawn from the fourth (2007-2008) and fifth (2014-2015) waves of the Indonesian Family Life Survey, including 7,964 and 7,986 adults aged 50 and older, respectively. HCU outcomes included outpatient service use and participation in the posyandu lansia programme during the previous four weeks. SEP was modelled as a latent construct based on education, household expenditure, and wealth indicators. Social networks were conceptualized across four ecological levels (individual, family, community, and societal), and social network dynamics were assessed using a panel sample of 4,411 individuals participating in both waves. Causal mediation analysis was applied to estimate the proportion of the SEP-HCU relationship mediated by social network characteristics. HCU was higher in IFLS-5 than in IFLS-4, with outpatient service use rising from 16.9% to 24.6% and posyandu participation from 2.5% to 6.7%. Socioeconomic inequalities persisted, with higher SEP groups more likely to use outpatient services. Social network dynamics were common, with 36% of respondents experiencing changes over time, and were socially patterned, with lower SEP groups more likely to show increasing network trajectories. Loneliness and marital status mediated up to 7.3% of the association between SEP and outpatient service use, while community-level social networks mediated up to 21.7% of the relationship with posyandu participation. Social networks contribute to explaining socioeconomic inequalities in HCU among older adults in Indonesia. The findings highlight the importance of both structural health system reforms and informal social support systems in shaping HCU. Strengthening community networks and addressing social isolation may complement improvements in formal healthcare coverage and help promote more equitable HCU in ageing populations.

The implementation of neurologic teleconsultations in rural primary care in Northeastern Germany (NeTKoH): an evaluation guided by the RE-AIM framework.

BMC health services research · 2026-11-12

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

Neurologic diseases represent a major public health burden both globally and nationally, straining the health care systems especially in rural areas with a scarcity of neurologists, and endangering patients' proximal access to care. The NeTKoH project (Neurologic Teleconsultation with General Practitioners to strengthen specialist care in Western Pomerania) addressed this by providing teleconsultations with neurologic experts from a university hospital for patients presenting with neurologic symptoms in primary care practices in rural areas of Western Pomerania in Northeast Germany. A comprehensive evaluation of such teleneurologic interventions under real-world conditions, focusing on their generalizability (both internal and external validity), is essential to advise decision-makers. We applied the Reach-Effectiveness-Adoption-Implementation-Maintenance (RE-AIM) framework to systematically assess NeTKoH's implementation and to inform its translation from research to practice. Each RE-AIM dimension was operationalized: &#x200b;Reach comprised the proportions of patients enrolled out of those approached by their physicians and of patients in the intervention who received a teleconsultation; Effectiveness measured the proportions of patients triaged to general practitioner care, of patients with improved QoL at follow-up, of same-day teleconsultations, and of positive patient responses regarding satisfaction; Adoption assessed the proportions of participating and of continuously participating physicians; Implementation focused on installed devices, preparatory visits with device training, device use by physicians, and teleconsultations performed; Maintenance measured patients' and physicians' self-reported intention to continue using teleconsultations. NeTKoH demonstrated high scores for Reach (98.8%), Adoption (83.0%), Implementation (99.2%), and Maintenance (85.0%), while Effectiveness was "generally positive" (66.0%). The representativeness of patient and physician populations in the study was limited, but both were broadly comparable to the regional target populations. Our findings suggest that teleconsultations for patients with neurologic conditions in primary care settings are feasible, acceptable, and improve access to specialist care. The RE-AIM framework provided relevant insights that can guide a dissemination trial for a potential scale-up. German Clinical Trials Register (DRKS00024492), registered on September 28, 2021.

Cue modality modulates dynamic functional network organization during motor imagery: Evidence from EEG functional connectivity and spatiotemporal decoding.

Biological psychology · 2026-11-12

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

Motor imagery (MI) is typically guided by external sensory cues; however, how cue modality and imagery type jointly influence functional connectivity during post-cue MI remains unclear. This study combined functional connectivity analysis with a CNN-TemporalConv-MLP spatiotemporal decoding model to investigate phase-locking value (PLV) differences across visual versus auditory cues and kinesthetic (KMI) versus visual (VMI) motor imagery. Functional connectivity analyses revealed that all connections with significant cue-modality effects in the early theta band showed higher PLV following visual cues. In the beta band, PLV was higher under visual cues in the earlier significant time window, whereas it was higher under auditory cues in the later significant time window. In contrast, differences between KMI and VMI were primarily localized to connections in the theta and alpha bands, while interactions between cue modality and imagery type emerged selectively in the theta, beta, and gamma bands. Decoding analyses showed that PLV functional connectivity features contained discriminative information about both factors. Overall cue-modality decoding achieved moderate performance, and performance improved when KMI and VMI conditions were held constant. By contrast, overall imagery-type classification showed relatively low discriminability. However, KMI versus VMI classification improved within specific cueing backgrounds, with better performance under fixed visual or auditory cues than under mixed cue-modality conditions. These findings indicate that the influence of cue modality can extend into subsequent MI, with functional connectivity differences showing selectivity across time, frequency, and connections. Condition-related differences were evident in both connection-specific PLV effects and deep learning decoding of PLV features.

Impact of hearing loss on visual selective attention: Evidence from N2pc and resting-state networks.

NeuroImage · 2026-11-05

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

Efficient visual attention is fundamental to environmental adaptation and social interaction. While previous studies have observed alterations in visual processing among deaf individuals, findings remain controversial regarding whether these changes stem from hearing loss (sensory compensation vs. cognitive deficit) or sign language experience. To address this issue, the present study provides task-related and resting-state electroencephalography (EEG) evidence to disentangle the specific drivers of visual selective attention plasticity. A visual search task was used to examine selective attention in 95 participants (34 prelingual deaf, 27 hearing signers, and 34 hearing nonsigners). Compared to both hearing groups, deaf individuals exhibited significantly lower hit rates and diminished N2-posterior contralateral (N2pc) amplitudes. Hearing signers and nonsigners showed comparable behavioral performance, N2pc amplitudes, and resting-state network topology, whereas the deaf group was further characterized by a degraded resting-state alpha network topology, evidenced by reduced clustering coefficients, global efficiency, and weakened frontal-occipital connectivity. N2pc amplitude correlated significantly with resting-state network properties and behavioral accuracy. These results suggest that deaf individuals may suffer from an imbalance in the regulation of visual selective attention, most clearly indexed by the evoked N2pc and by intrinsic alpha-network organization, and likely linked to increased resource competition following the loss of auditory monitoring. Our findings provide neurophysiological evidence that these attentional deficits are associated with hearing loss rather than with sign-language experience, offering a new perspective for understanding the boundaries of cross-modal plasticity in the human brain.

Spectral-spatial modeling of neural couplings in functional connectivity of motor networks.

NeuroImage · 2026-11-05

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

Functional neural connectivity reflects coordinated interactions within brain networks in both health and disease. Phase-amplitude coupling (PAC) is widely used for this purpose, but its pairwise formulations overlook spatially distributed, spectrally correlated structures in neural activity that arises from complex brain circuits. We developed a spectral-spatial generalized linear model (SS-GLM) to characterize the high-frequency response as the integrated outcome of multiple low-frequency phase sources across channels and frequencies. Our method also utilizes a regularization strategy to mitigate predictor collinearity and spurious effects for stable attribution of network-level coupling estimates. Validation was performed using both synthetic data and human motor network recordings from 25 patients with Parkinson's disease undergoing deep brain stimulation (DBS) surgery. In synthetic analyses, SS-GLM accurately recovered ground-truth coupling configurations under multiple plausible scenarios. In real data, the framework revealed coherent cortico-subthalamic coupling patterns across distinct motor tasks that aligned with clinically effective DBS contacts and their anatomical organization. Conventional pairwise PAC metrics, such as the modulation index and GLM assess interactions in isolation. In contrast, SS-GLM jointly models distributed predictors and yields spatially localized, spectrally specific coupling estimates that also achieve stronger correspondence with clinical stimulation outcomes. This work offers a principled and extensible framework for advancing functional connectivity analyses, with improved interpretability of network-level interactions, enhanced robustness to confounds, and broad potential for future applications in systems neuroscience and neuro-engineering research.

Digital twin control framework uncovers individualized cross-scale links between functional connectivity deviations, communication impairment, and molecular mechanisms in autism.

NeuroImage · 2026-11-05

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) is hallmarked by pronounced inter-individual heterogeneity in brain function and clinical manifestations. Conventional neuroimaging studies yield highly inconsistent findings, as extrinsic confounding factors and intrinsic disease heterogeneity mask ASD-specific functional connectivity (FC) signatures, hampering the identification of individualized neural mechanisms linked to symptom severity. We developed a digital twin control analytical framework based on deep contrastive learning. By comparing each ASD participant with their matched digital twin control, we quantified individual-specific FC deviations. Principal component analysis was applied to extract the core aberrant pattern, followed by multi-dimensional analyzes and validation including clinical correlation, transcriptomic decoding, neurotransmitter and mitochondrial co-localization, as well as disease and cognitive enrichment. The model effectively disentangled disease&#x2011;related from non&#x2011;disease&#x2011;related variations. The identified dominant aberrant FC pattern was significantly associated with Autism Diagnostic Observation Schedule (ADOS) communication scores, suggesting it is associated with to communication impairment. This pattern showed convergent associations with synaptic pathways, excitatory/inhibitory neuronal signatures, critical developmental windows and subcortical structures, and exhibited negative spatial association with multiple neurotransmitter systems and mitochondrial respiratory complexes. Biological validation further supported its ASD specificity and communication&#x2011;related cognitive relevance. Leveraging the twin control analytical framework, this study provides an exploratory cross-scale framework linking individualized FC deviations with clinical, transcriptomic, neurochemical, and mitochondrial maps. The findings indicate that communication difficulties in ASD may partly arise from multi-scale disturbances in neural integration, social-perceptual processing and activity-dependent cellular energy regulation.

Decoding individual differences in mental information from human brain responses predicted by deep neural networks.

NeuroImage · 2026-11-05

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

Brain decoding using functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) has high potential for evaluating unobservable mental information that varies across individuals in everyday situations. Although estimating individual differences in mental information using brain decoding could be effective for social applications, especially in neuromarketing, the physical constraints and high costs of fMRI limit the practical application in real-world settings. To overcome these limitations, we propose a framework to estimate individual differences in decodable mental information using brain responses predicted by deep neural networks (DNNs). In this framework, DNN-based models are trained to predict fMRI responses to natural movie scenes. Then, additional fMRI measurements are no longer required because mental information for new movie scenes can be decoded from the predicted responses instead of the measured ones. A large-scale analysis revealed that the proposed predicted-response decoding captures individual difference patterns consistent with those derived from conventional measured-response decoding across various cognitive domains. Of the 88 cognitive labels associated with movie scenes, individual difference patterns could be captured for more than 70% of the labels. This framework offers a promising approach for evaluating personal mental information with substantially reduced reliance on fMRI, thereby expanding the potential for social applications of brain decoding.

Do neuroimaging data have higher-order interactions?

NeuroImage · 2026-11-05

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

Recent advances in the physics of complex systems have motivated the study of higher-order structure in neuroimaging data, including functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI), electroencephalography (EEG), and magnetoencephalography (MEG). Despite the proliferation of proposed measures, the meaning of "higher-order" is often left implicit, and the term is used inconsistently across studies. In this work, we clarify the distinction between higher-order interactions and higher-order dependencies. Higher-order interactions necessarily imply higher-order dependencies, but the converse does not hold: systems can exhibit higher-order dependencies in the absence of higher-order interactions, as in the case of Gaussian variables. We present several equivalent definitions of higher-order interactions for continuous data, illustrate them using simple model systems, and use them to assess the specificity of measures commonly applied in neuroimaging. We show that these measures are not specific to higher-order interactions. Consequently, although existing studies support the presence of higher-order dependencies in neuroimaging data, they do not provide conclusive evidence for the presence of higher-order interactions.
