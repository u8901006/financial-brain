Effects of State Paid Sick Leave Mandates on Children's Mental Healthcare Utilization.

Health economics · 2026-11-19

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

Many major mental health conditions first emerge in childhood, when early intervention can produce both immediate and long-term benefits. The United States has no federal paid sick leave (PSL) policy, but 18 states have implemented PSL mandates that give employees job-protected, paid time off to address their own health needs or those of a dependent. We study the effects of these mandates on children's mental healthcare use, combining data from the 2016 to 2024 National Survey of Children's Health with difference-in-differences and event-study methods. We find suggestive evidence that children's mental healthcare use increases following PSL mandate implementation, though the difference-in-differences estimate is imprecise. Event-study analyses suggest a short-run increase in use that dissipates over time. The increases are particularly pronounced among children ages 5 to 11. These findings suggest that PSL mandates may help parents obtain mental healthcare for their children and make other investments in their children.
