Aging Out of Coverage: Lessons From the West Virginia $1,000 Adult Dental Benefit.

Health economics · 2026-10-22

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

This paper studies the utilization of dental services during the recent implementation of an adult (age 21 and over) Medicaid dental benefit in West Virginia. We analyze claims data and find: (1) before implementation, quarterly utilization of preventative and restorative services falls from 8% at age 20 to virtually 0% at age 21; (2) post-implementation, utilization falls from 8% at age 20 to 5% at age 21, implying a net increase of 50% of baseline utilization; (3) the net effect of the dental benefit is significantly larger in counties with a larger number of oral health care provider practices. Our results provide evidence that public health insurance expansions are dampened in markets with a limited supply of provider practices.

Work-time arrangements, career progression, and job satisfaction among physicians in Germany: a cross-sectional survey.

BMC health services research · 2026-10-22

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

Demographic change and evolving expectations regarding work-life balance are increasingly challenging the medical workforce in Germany. Work-time arrangements play a key role in physician job satisfaction, well-being, and career development. However, part-time and alternative working models remain unevenly implemented and are frequently perceived as limiting career opportunities. This study characterized current work-time arrangements among physicians in Germany and examined satisfaction, employer support, colleague acceptance, perceived career opportunities, barriers to reducing working hours, and work-family balance. This cross-sectional study is based on an anonymous digital survey conducted by the German Medical Women's Association between September 2022 and July 2023. Physicians across medical specialties and career stages were invited to participate. The questionnaire assessed sociodemographic and professional characteristics, work-time arrangements (full-time, part-time, and alternative models), satisfaction with current arrangements, employer support, and perceived career opportunities. Of the 898 respondents, 90.9% were women and 46.2% were resident physicians. Full-time employment was the most common arrangement (56.8%). Physicians working in non-full-time arrangements were more frequently satisfied with their work-time arrangement than those working full-time (52.8% vs. 45.3%; p&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.020). Hospital-based physicians were less likely to be satisfied with their work-time arrangement (aOR 0.33) or to receive support from their employer in implementing alternative work-time arrangements (aOR 0.24). They were also less likely to report acceptance of alternative work-time arrangements among colleagues (aOR 0.21) and equal career opportunities under such arrangements (aOR 0.31), while being more likely to report difficulties balancing work and family responsibilities (aOR 2.44; all p&#x2009;<&#x2009;0.001). Compared with physicians in leadership positions, resident physicians and board-certified physicians without leadership responsibility reported lower satisfaction and lower perceived equal career opportunities, while reporting greater difficulty balancing work and family responsibilities (all p&#x2009;<&#x2009;0.001). Fear of negative career consequences was the most frequently reported reason for not reducing working hours (58.2%), and approximately two thirds of respondents perceived unequal career opportunities for colleagues using alternative work-time arrangements. Although full-time employment remained the most common arrangement, respondents not working full-time reported greater satisfaction. Part-time and alternative work-time-arrangements are widely perceived as career-limiting. Addressing structural barriers and ensuring equal career opportunities for physicians who require or prefer family-friendly working conditions are essential for physician well-being, workforce retention, and the long-term resilience of healthcare systems.

Statistical Learning of Feature-Spatial Mappings and Its Modulation by Cue-Induced Expectation.

Biological psychology · 2026-10-22

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

Visual statistical learning enables individuals to extract regularities from the environment, thereby optimizing attentional allocation. Previous research has mainly focused on unidimensional location or feature probability learning, leaving unclear whether individuals can acquire multidimensional mappings between target features and high-probability spatial locations, and how cue-induced expectations modulate the neural expression of such mappings. We employed a location probability paradigm combined with multivariate pattern analysis (MVPA) and recorded EEG. Reaction times showed a significant gradient from the feature-matched high-probability location to the low-probability location, indicating successful acquisition of feature-spatial mappings under both cue conditions. The N2pc did not reliably differentiate the two high-probability location conditions, providing no detectable evidence of feature-dependent modulation of early lateralized selection. Predictive cues elicited brief pretarget alpha lateralization toward the cue-associated HPTL. Search-array-locked MVPA discriminated feature-matched from feature-mismatched HPTL trials under both cue conditions, with stronger decoding under predictive cueing. These findings show that learned feature-spatial regularities were expressed behaviorally and in distributed neural activity, and that predictive cues modulated their neural expression before and after search-array onset.
