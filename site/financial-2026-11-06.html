Bridging the evidence gap: a qualitative COM-B analysis of facilitators and barriers to evaluating heat-health action plans in Germany.

BMC health services research · 2026-11-03

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

Extreme heat and heat waves are increasing in intensity, duration, and frequency. The World Health Organization recommends the development of heat-health action plans (HHAPs) for public health protection. In Germany, districts and federal states have started to implement HHAPs. However, reports on ongoing evaluations as well as evidence on the effectiveness and efficiency of planned or implemented measures are scarce. We aimed to explore the perspectives of professionals in heat and health protection on current evaluation practices, focusing on perceived facilitators of and barriers to the evaluation of HHAPs at different stages. We interviewed 23 professionals with expertise or leadership responsibility from the field of heat and health working in public administration at the district, federal state, and national level, or nongovernmental organizations (NGOs) throughout Germany. By conducting thematic analysis following a hybrid combination of deductive and inductive coding within the COM-B model, we derived and categorized facilitators of and barriers to evaluating HHAPs. Additionally, we clustered them into individual, organizational, and policy levels based on the Socioecological Model. Although all interviewees perceived the evaluation of HHAPs as important, evaluation procedures were rarely systematically realized in practice. Interviewees emphasized the importance of opportunities such as political support as well as a clear mandate and assignment for evaluation. Moreover, structural conditions, like the absence of guidelines and standards for evaluation, data infrastructure, personnel, and funding, were reported to often hinder evaluation, resulting in limited experience and knowledge in evaluating complex interventions such as HHAPs. Collaboration and networks were reported as facilitative in process evaluation. While individual motivation was not reported as a reason for not evaluating HHAPs, a combination of identified barriers often contributed to feeling overwhelmed with the task of evaluation. Our findings suggest that evaluation is not consistently institutionalized in German heat-health action planning. Barriers to evaluating HHAPs are not primarily related to individual or district-level motivation, but rather limited by opportunity and capability shaped within political and institutional conditions. To institutionalize HHAP evaluation, greater political commitment, supportive structures, data infrastructures, and guidance for evaluation, as well as targeted funding and personnel are required.

Facilitators and barriers to the early implementation of speaking valves in intensive care: a qualitative study using normalisation process theory.

BMC health services research · 2026-11-04

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

The utilization of tracheostomy in conjunction with mechanical ventilation significantly impacts various physiological functions, particularly the ability to communicate verbally. Speaking valves, integrated into the ventilator circuit, restore phonation and are recognised internationally as best practice, yet remain underused in Swedish intensive care units (ICUs). Beyond enabling verbal communication, the early use of speaking valves has been associated with improved swallowing, secretion management, and reduced duration of mechanical ventilation. The TRACH INSPIRE project aims to promote their implementation in Swedish ICUs. This study explored facilitators and barriers to the early interprofessional implementation of speaking valves in tracheostomised, mechanically ventilated ICU patients. This qualitative study design analysed 19 semi-structured interview data, pre-implementation, with ICU professionals (intensivists, nurses, physiotherapists, and speech-language pathologists) from two participating ICUs in the TRACH INSPIRE project conducted in January-February 2025. Inclusion criteria were that the participants must have participated in the interprofessional competency training for the TRACH INSPIRE project. No formal exclusion criteria were defined. The Data were analysed using deductive content analysis guided by the Normalisation Process Theory (NPT), which examines how new practices are embedded into routine care. Twelve subcategories were identified that were organized according to the NPT. Facilitators included a shared understanding of the intervention's value, strong professional commitment, structured implementation processes, managerial support, clearly defined roles, effective interprofessional collaboration, targeted training, and open communication. Barriers centred on organisational constraints such as limited resources, low patient volumes, lack of a comprehensive implementation strategy, and documentation-related challenges. Early implementation of speaking valves in mechanically ventilated, tracheostomised ICU patients is supported by high interprofessional engagement, shared recognition of benefits, and structured training. However, operational limitations, small eligible patient populations, and documentation burdens present notable challenges. Inclusive team strategies, maintaining competencies, and streamlined processes may promote sustainable integration into routine ICU practice.

Barriers to and facilitators of gestational diabetes mellitus service utilization in Kenya: a qualitative analysis of women's and healthcare workers' perspectives.

BMC health services research · 2026-11-04

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

Gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) is a growing global public health concern, particularly in low- and middle-income countries such as Kenya. Despite rising prevalence and associated maternal and neonatal risks, timely diagnosis and comprehensive GDM care remain limited by systemic, socioeconomic, and cultural barriers. This study identified barriers and facilitators influencing GDM care provision and utilization in Kisumu County, Kenya, from the perspectives of healthcare workers (HCWs) and women affected by GDM. A qualitative content analysis combined semistructured interviews and structured surveys. Participants were purposively sampled from two healthcare facilities in Kisumu County: Jaramogi Oginga Odinga Teaching and Referral Hospital and Kisumu Specialists Hospital. Interviews involved eight HCWs and two women diagnosed with GDM, and surveys were administered to eleven women with a history of GDM. Data collection instruments, developed from the health needs assessment and knowledge, attitude, and behaviour frameworks, were validated by an expert panel from Kenya and Germany. Thematic analysis identified key barriers to and facilitators of GDM care. Stakeholders highlighted systemic gaps and resource constraints affecting GDM care, including high costs, inadequate insurance, supply shortages, and poor infrastructure, which especially affected rural and low-income women. Barriers including financial burden, distance, inconsistent protocols, communication gaps, and negative patient-provider relationships hindered access and adherence. Facilitators included family and social support, health worker education and follow-up, and self-management through dietary counseling, monitoring, and lifestyle changes. All groups advocated universal screening, expanded insurance, community outreach, and better education to strengthen GDM care and improve outcomes. GDM care provision and utilization in Kenya are shaped by multilevel barriers spanning individual, systemic, and cultural domains. Addressing them requires targeted interventions, including enhanced HCW training, greater community awareness, and strengthened health system infrastructure. Integrating GDM services into existing maternal health programmes is essential to improving outcomes for women and their infants in resource-constrained settings.

Preprimary education laws and women's gender attitudes, empowerment and reproductive autonomy in 117 low- and middle-income countries.

Nature human behaviour · 2026-11-04

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

Preprimary education is central to the global education agenda, including Sustainable Development Goal 4.2. Despite global momentum in low- and middle-income countries, cross-national evidence on long-term outcomes associated with preprimary education legislation remains limited. Here we applied a matrix-completion approach with subgroup and sensitivity analyses using 495 nationally representative surveys from 1990 to 2024, covering nearly 6.7&#x2009;million women across 117 countries. Eligibility under the legislation was associated with higher educational enrolment and completion, and with more gender-egalitarian attitudes, more favourable empowerment-related behaviours and greater reproductive autonomy. For example, eligibility was associated with higher female primary-school enrolment (1.21 percentage points (pp); 95% confidence interval (CI) 0.52 to 1.91; P&#x2009;<&#x2009;0.001) and completion (0.75 pp; 95% CI 0.24 to 1.25; P&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.004), greater disapproval of wife-beating (1.04 pp; 95% CI 0.53 to 1.56; P&#x2009;<&#x2009;0.001), greater participation in decisions about husbands' earnings (3.25 pp; 95% CI 2.50 to 4.00; P&#x2009;<&#x2009;0.001) and greater ever use of contraception (2.96 pp; 95% CI 2.33 to 3.59; P&#x2009;<&#x2009;0.001). Eligibility under the legislation was also associated with lower rates of adolescent childbearing (-2.41 pp; 95% CI -2.67 to -2.14; P&#x2009;<&#x2009;0.001) and child marriage (-0.43 pp; 95% CI -0.71 to -0.15; P&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.003). These findings provide cross-national evidence directly relevant to ongoing policy discussions surrounding Sustainable Development Goal 4.2 and broader global development agendas, while emphasizing the importance of implementation capacity and equitable system expansion.

Understanding Audiovisual Affective Processing: Behavioral Effects and Neural Underpinnings.

Social cognitive and affective neuroscience · 2026-11-05

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

Emotional signals are typically conveyed jointly through vision and audition. This article reviews behavioral, electrophysiological, and neuroimaging evidence on audiovisual affective processing, addressing what its core effects are, when they unfold, and where they occur in the brain. Congruent audiovisual cues often facilitate emotion recognition, although benefits vary across emotions, tasks, measures, and unimodal comparisons. Audiovisual input can also shape affective appraisals and subjective emotional experience. Modality dominance is context-dependent, varying with cue reliability and the affective dimension judged. Cross-modal influences can persist when one modality is task-irrelevant, suggesting limited goal dependence. Electrophysiological findings reveal audiovisual affective interactions at both early and later stages of neural processing. Neuroimaging work implicates posterior superior temporal cortex (pSTC) within a distributed network of sensory, subcortical, and frontal regions. Building on this evidence, we consider Anderson's information integration theory as a computational framework for characterizing modality-related and value-dependent contributions to affective judgments. We propose a hypothesis-generating dual-pathway framework predicting that reliability-related changes in cue influence will covary with sensory-pSTC interactions, whereas value-dependent changes will additionally involve amygdala and prefrontal systems. These hypotheses concern relative rather than exclusive network contributions and require direct testing through independent manipulations of cue reliability and affective value.

Impact of hearing loss on visual selective attention: Evidence from N2pc and resting-state networks.

NeuroImage · 2026-11-05

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

Efficient visual attention is fundamental to environmental adaptation and social interaction. While previous studies have observed alterations in visual processing among deaf individuals, findings remain controversial regarding whether these changes stem from hearing loss (sensory compensation vs. cognitive deficit) or sign language experience. To address this issue, the present study provides task-related and resting-state electroencephalography (EEG) evidence to disentangle the specific drivers of visual selective attention plasticity. A visual search task was used to examine selective attention in 95 participants (34 prelingual deaf, 27 hearing signers, and 34 hearing nonsigners). Compared to both hearing groups, deaf individuals exhibited significantly lower hit rates and diminished N2-posterior contralateral (N2pc) amplitudes. Hearing signers and nonsigners showed comparable behavioral performance, N2pc amplitudes, and resting-state network topology, whereas the deaf group was further characterized by a degraded resting-state alpha network topology, evidenced by reduced clustering coefficients, global efficiency, and weakened frontal-occipital connectivity. N2pc amplitude correlated significantly with resting-state network properties and behavioral accuracy. These results suggest that deaf individuals may suffer from an imbalance in the regulation of visual selective attention, most clearly indexed by the evoked N2pc and by intrinsic alpha-network organization, and likely linked to increased resource competition following the loss of auditory monitoring. Our findings provide neurophysiological evidence that these attentional deficits are associated with hearing loss rather than with sign-language experience, offering a new perspective for understanding the boundaries of cross-modal plasticity in the human brain.

Spectral-spatial modeling of neural couplings in functional connectivity of motor networks.

NeuroImage · 2026-11-05

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

Functional neural connectivity reflects coordinated interactions within brain networks in both health and disease. Phase-amplitude coupling (PAC) is widely used for this purpose, but its pairwise formulations overlook spatially distributed, spectrally correlated structures in neural activity that arises from complex brain circuits. We developed a spectral-spatial generalized linear model (SS-GLM) to characterize the high-frequency response as the integrated outcome of multiple low-frequency phase sources across channels and frequencies. Our method also utilizes a regularization strategy to mitigate predictor collinearity and spurious effects for stable attribution of network-level coupling estimates. Validation was performed using both synthetic data and human motor network recordings from 25 patients with Parkinson's disease undergoing deep brain stimulation (DBS) surgery. In synthetic analyses, SS-GLM accurately recovered ground-truth coupling configurations under multiple plausible scenarios. In real data, the framework revealed coherent cortico-subthalamic coupling patterns across distinct motor tasks that aligned with clinically effective DBS contacts and their anatomical organization. Conventional pairwise PAC metrics, such as the modulation index and GLM assess interactions in isolation. In contrast, SS-GLM jointly models distributed predictors and yields spatially localized, spectrally specific coupling estimates that also achieve stronger correspondence with clinical stimulation outcomes. This work offers a principled and extensible framework for advancing functional connectivity analyses, with improved interpretability of network-level interactions, enhanced robustness to confounds, and broad potential for future applications in systems neuroscience and neuro-engineering research.

Digital twin control framework uncovers individualized cross-scale links between functional connectivity deviations, communication impairment, and molecular mechanisms in autism.

NeuroImage · 2026-11-05

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) is hallmarked by pronounced inter-individual heterogeneity in brain function and clinical manifestations. Conventional neuroimaging studies yield highly inconsistent findings, as extrinsic confounding factors and intrinsic disease heterogeneity mask ASD-specific functional connectivity (FC) signatures, hampering the identification of individualized neural mechanisms linked to symptom severity. We developed a digital twin control analytical framework based on deep contrastive learning. By comparing each ASD participant with their matched digital twin control, we quantified individual-specific FC deviations. Principal component analysis was applied to extract the core aberrant pattern, followed by multi-dimensional analyzes and validation including clinical correlation, transcriptomic decoding, neurotransmitter and mitochondrial co-localization, as well as disease and cognitive enrichment. The model effectively disentangled disease&#x2011;related from non&#x2011;disease&#x2011;related variations. The identified dominant aberrant FC pattern was significantly associated with Autism Diagnostic Observation Schedule (ADOS) communication scores, suggesting it is associated with to communication impairment. This pattern showed convergent associations with synaptic pathways, excitatory/inhibitory neuronal signatures, critical developmental windows and subcortical structures, and exhibited negative spatial association with multiple neurotransmitter systems and mitochondrial respiratory complexes. Biological validation further supported its ASD specificity and communication&#x2011;related cognitive relevance. Leveraging the twin control analytical framework, this study provides an exploratory cross-scale framework linking individualized FC deviations with clinical, transcriptomic, neurochemical, and mitochondrial maps. The findings indicate that communication difficulties in ASD may partly arise from multi-scale disturbances in neural integration, social-perceptual processing and activity-dependent cellular energy regulation.

Decoding individual differences in mental information from human brain responses predicted by deep neural networks.

NeuroImage · 2026-11-05

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

Brain decoding using functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) has high potential for evaluating unobservable mental information that varies across individuals in everyday situations. Although estimating individual differences in mental information using brain decoding could be effective for social applications, especially in neuromarketing, the physical constraints and high costs of fMRI limit the practical application in real-world settings. To overcome these limitations, we propose a framework to estimate individual differences in decodable mental information using brain responses predicted by deep neural networks (DNNs). In this framework, DNN-based models are trained to predict fMRI responses to natural movie scenes. Then, additional fMRI measurements are no longer required because mental information for new movie scenes can be decoded from the predicted responses instead of the measured ones. A large-scale analysis revealed that the proposed predicted-response decoding captures individual difference patterns consistent with those derived from conventional measured-response decoding across various cognitive domains. Of the 88 cognitive labels associated with movie scenes, individual difference patterns could be captured for more than 70% of the labels. This framework offers a promising approach for evaluating personal mental information with substantially reduced reliance on fMRI, thereby expanding the potential for social applications of brain decoding.

Do neuroimaging data have higher-order interactions?

NeuroImage · 2026-11-05

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

Recent advances in the physics of complex systems have motivated the study of higher-order structure in neuroimaging data, including functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI), electroencephalography (EEG), and magnetoencephalography (MEG). Despite the proliferation of proposed measures, the meaning of "higher-order" is often left implicit, and the term is used inconsistently across studies. In this work, we clarify the distinction between higher-order interactions and higher-order dependencies. Higher-order interactions necessarily imply higher-order dependencies, but the converse does not hold: systems can exhibit higher-order dependencies in the absence of higher-order interactions, as in the case of Gaussian variables. We present several equivalent definitions of higher-order interactions for continuous data, illustrate them using simple model systems, and use them to assess the specificity of measures commonly applied in neuroimaging. We show that these measures are not specific to higher-order interactions. Consequently, although existing studies support the presence of higher-order dependencies in neuroimaging data, they do not provide conclusive evidence for the presence of higher-order interactions.
