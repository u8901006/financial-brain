The Impact of Repealing Certificate-of-Need Laws on Healthcare Spending: A Causal Analysis.

Health economics · 2026-10-15

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

Certificate-of-Need laws in 35 US states require healthcare providers to obtain government approval before building new facilities or expanding services. Their stated goal is to ensure a genuine community need and avoid redundant services, with the aim of controlling healthcare costs and distributing resources fairly, although their effectiveness is debated. Our study differs from previous literature as we present the causal effects of repealing Certificate-of-Need laws on various healthcare expenses using modern difference-in-differences techniques and event studies. Our findings suggest that repealing Certificate-of-Need laws can reduce the burden of healthcare spending.

Impaired Neural Pattern Separation During Implicit Emotion Regulation in PTSD: An MVPA fMRI Study.

Social cognitive and affective neuroscience · 2026-10-15

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

Implicit emotion regulation (IER) operates automatically and supports adaptive social-affective functioning. Although PTSD involves marked IER disturbances, the large-scale functional connectivity architecture supporting implicit emotional processing remains unclear. The Shifted Attention Emotion Appraisal Task (SEAT) offers an ecologically relevant framework for probing these processes. One hundred twenty-three adults (PTSD: n = 78; trauma-exposed controls: n = 45) completed fMRI during SEAT. Whole-brain functional connectivity multivariate pattern analysis (fc-MVPA) identified data-driven, condition-specific connectivity patterns distinguishing groups. MVPA-derived clusters were further assessed using seed-based and ROI-to-ROI analyses. fc-MVPA revealed three frontal-anchored connectivity patterns spanning frontoparietal, salience, and executive networks. Seed-based analyses centered on the right frontal pole showed increased coupling with posterior default mode and visual regions and reduced connectivity with frontal control systems. Dysconnectivity varied across regulatory stages, including frontal-occipital hyperconnectivity during threat-related attention shifts and frontal-midline hypoconnectivity during neutral appraisal. Greater re-experiencing severity was nominally associated with reduced multivariate network strength during implicit processing and appraisal; however, these associations did not survive correction for multiple comparisons and should be considered exploratory. Implicit regulation deficits in PTSD reflect distributed network dysfunction rather than localized abnormalities. Disrupted coordination among control, perceptual, and self-referential systems may limit flexible emotional responses. Integrating SEAT with fc-MVPA identifies connectivity patterns relevant to maladaptive emotional processing and advances network-level models of dysregulation in PTSD.
