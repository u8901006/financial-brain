Receipt of basic public health services and social participation among rural migrants in China: a cross-sectional study.

BMC health services research · 2026-11-07

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

Social participation is an important behavioral dimension through which rural migrants enter urban public life and form social ties. Basic public health services may have social associations that extend beyond health management. This study compared the associations of health record management and health education with social participation among rural migrants and examined population differences and three potential explanatory factors. Data were drawn from the 2017 China Migrants Dynamic Survey and included 127,037 migrants with agricultural hukou who had lived at their destination for more than 6 months. Health record management was used to represent an administrative service centered on registration and inclusion in management, whereas health education represented a substantive service centered on knowledge provision and behavioral guidance. Baseline analyses used ordinary least squares models with city fixed effects and city-clustered robust standard errors. Leave-one-out service receipt rates at the village or residents' committee level were used in supplementary two-stage least squares sensitivity analyses. Oster omitted-variable sensitivity analysis, alternative-outcome tests, restricted-sample tests, exploratory heterogeneity analyses, and analyses of potential explanatory factors were also conducted. After full adjustment, the coefficients for health record management and health education in relation to the social participation index were 0.0176 (SE&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.0017) and 0.0293 (SE&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.0012), respectively, corresponding to 0.16 and 0.26 standard deviations of the outcome. The corresponding coefficients from the supplementary two-stage least squares sensitivity analyses were 0.0274 (SE&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.0040) and 0.0431 (SE&#x2009;=&#x2009;0.0025). Other sensitivity and robustness analyses produced directionally consistent results. Between-group tests based on seemingly unrelated estimation showed that the positive association between health education and social participation was larger among men, respondents born in or after 1980, and respondents with a high school education or above. Adjusted analyses also showed that both health record management and health education were positively associated with hygiene-habit similarity, local identity, and local integration intention. All three potential explanatory factors were also positively associated with social participation, although the magnitudes of these associations were small. Receipt of basic public health services was associated with higher levels of social participation among rural migrants, and the magnitude of the association was larger for health education than for health record management. The findings suggest that primary-level public health services may have social value related to institutional contact, information access, and community participation in addition to their health management functions. Policy practice could consider improving the effective reach of health education, using health records as an entry point for continuous services, and attending to differences in service associations across sex, birth-cohort, and education groups.

Exploring challenges related to health literacy of Albanian immigrants in Germany: a qualitative study among a younger, educated cohort.

BMC health services research · 2026-11-07

分類：Fallback

中文摘要

Albanian immigrants in Germany face health literacy challenges due to language barriers, cultural differences, and unfamiliarity with the healthcare system, which intersect with systemic issues such as medical staff shortages. This qualitative study explored the health literacy and health challenges of Albanian immigrants in Germany, examining their capacity to navigate healthcare services, make informed decisions, and identify barriers and facilitators influencing their experiences. Semi-structured interviews were conducted with 15 Albanian immigrants (mean age 27; predominantly younger and highly educated). Participants were recruited via online communities and cultural centers. Data were analyzed using thematic content analysis guided by thematic saturation principles. Participants reported multiple barriers to healthcare access. Language proficiency gaps impeded accurate communication of medical concerns, with several describing difficulty understanding diagnoses, treatment plans, and medical terminology. Bureaucratic delays and long waiting times for appointments deterred timely care utilization, and securing a family doctor proved challenging depending on region of residence. Despite these obstacles, participants expressed strong trust in physicians and satisfaction with treatment quality once received. Statutory health insurance mitigated financial concerns for most. Systemic strains, particularly staff shortages, exacerbated communication gaps and care delays, with physician participants acknowledging workforce deficits contribute to prolonged waiting periods. Shared decision-making was inconsistently practiced; some reported needing to insist on referrals. Experiences of friends and relatives further shaped expectations, with accounts of misdiagnosis due to language barriers reinforcing concerns about systemic responsiveness. Socioeconomic factors, including education level, employment status, and insurance coverage, shaped participants' healthcare experiences, with higher education and statutory insurance mitigating financial concerns, yet systemic barriers such as bureaucratic delays, communication gaps and provider shortages persisted regardless of socioeconomic position. Despite these barriers, participants maintained trust in the system, valuing statutory health insurance, quality treatment, and provider professionalism.
